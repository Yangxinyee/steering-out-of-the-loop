You are going to repair a trained world model by editing its weights.

THE SETTING
The model is an event-stream world model of ICU stays, trained on records from a simulated ICU (no patient data). It reads
a stay's record (glucose and mean arterial pressure (MAP) readings, insulin and norepinephrine infusions) and predicts what
will be recorded next. It was trained by maximum likelihood on 30,000 stays.

We use it to answer "what if" questions about treatment by rolling it forward under a changed dose. For example: if every
insulin infusion from ICU hour 12 on had run at twice the recorded rate, how would glucose in hours 13 to 24 have differed?
A model that predicts the record well can still answer such questions badly. In a record, doses follow the patient's
condition, so a model can learn how doses and readings go together without learning what the drugs do.

YOUR TASK
Edit the model's weights so that its answers to these dose questions match what the drugs really do in this ICU, while its
factual predictions stay as good as they are now. You have to establish what the drugs really do from the training records;
nothing else will tell you. The true effects may be large, small, or absent. Base every conclusion on the records, not on
medical knowledge: this simulated ICU need not behave like a real one.

HOW YOUR EDIT WILL BE JUDGED
After you finish, your edited model is evaluated on stays you never see, from the same ICU, with exactly the rollout that the
forward tool's dose_edit request runs:
1. Main readout: insulin x2 from hour 12. The predicted mean change in glucose_finger over hours 13-24 (at the stay-hours
   after insulin had run), compared with the true change.
2. Also reported, in the same way: insulin x1/2 on glucose_finger, and norepinephrine x2 and x1/2 on map_arterial.
3. Factual fit: the value NLL per reading (teacher-forced, as the factual request reports it) must not get worse than the
   original model's by more than 0.005 nats.
The edit is judged on how close the four readouts come to the true effects, provided the factual fit holds.
